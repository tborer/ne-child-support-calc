    Enter children's health insurance premiums and how many overnights each parent has per year.

Nebraska child support FAQ

How is child support calculated in Nebraska?

Nebraska uses an income-shares model. Each parent's monthly net income (gross income minus allowed deductions such as taxes, FICA, mandatory retirement and support for other children) is combined. The combined net income and number of children are used to find the basic support amount in the Table 1 schedule. Health insurance premiums for the children are added, and the total obligation is split between the parents in proportion to their share of combined net income.

What is the difference between Worksheet 1 and Joint Physical Custody (Worksheet 3)?

Worksheet 1 is the basic net income calculation, typically used when one parent has the children most of the time. The Joint Physical Custody calculation (Worksheet 3) applies when parenting time is shared more evenly: the Table 1 amount is multiplied by 1.5 and each parent's share is weighted by the other parent's percentage of overnights, then the two obligations are offset.

When does Nebraska use the joint physical custody calculation?

Under the Nebraska Child Support Guidelines, the joint physical custody worksheet is presumed to apply when a joint physical custody arrangement is ordered and each parent has the children for more than 142 days per year. This calculator also warns you if the time split you enter falls outside a 30%–70% range for either parent.

What deductions from gross income are allowed?

Common deductions include federal and state income taxes, FICA (Social Security and Medicare), mandatory retirement contributions, previously ordered child support, regular support for other children, and health insurance premiums for the parent. The calculator's Deduction Calculator totals these for each parent.

How does health insurance affect Nebraska child support?

The children's health insurance premium is added to the basic support amount, divided between the parents by their income percentages, and the parent who actually pays the premium receives a credit. The calculator flags premiums that exceed 3% of a parent's gross income.

Is my information stored or sent anywhere?

Your numbers are never stored. Running totals are calculated in your browser as you type. When you finalize, the figures are sent securely to compute your result and then discarded. Payment is handled by Stripe, so card details never reach this site.

How much does it cost?

Entering your numbers, seeing the running totals and using the Table 1 and deduction tools is free. Finalizing a calculation is a one-time fee, paid securely through Stripe. There is no subscription and no account to create.

Is this calculator a substitute for a lawyer or a court order?

No. Results are estimates for informational purposes and are not legal advice. Courts can deviate from the guidelines, and your final support amount is set by a court order. Consult a licensed Nebraska attorney and the official Nebraska Supreme Court worksheets for your case.
